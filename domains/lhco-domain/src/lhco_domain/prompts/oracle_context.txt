Domain: collider analysis (LHCO format).

HELPER_REGISTRY notes:
Each helper has name, section, summary, keywords, aliases, and dependencies.
Pay special attention to helpers with overlapping keywords or aliases,
as these are the most likely sources of unstable helper selection.

Focus especially on ambiguities such as:

- Whether an object means a selected object or a raw event-level object.
  Example: "the leading object" may mean the leading object among selected objects after cuts,
  or the leading object in the full uncleaned collection.

- Whether a veto or count is applied to selected objects or all event-level objects.
  Example: "veto events with more than N objects" may mean selected objects or all objects.

- Whether a phrase refers to a reconstructed composite object or only a scalar quantity.
  Example: "the two-object system" may mean a single reconstructed composite object,
  or only a scalar quantity derived from it.

- Whether validation histograms are filled after the full event selection or as soon as the observable becomes available.

- Whether leading/subleading objects are defined before or after selection cuts.

- Whether the same physical object is used consistently across selection cuts, validation plots, and output.

- When multiple helpers in the registry can compute a similar quantity on different object collections,
  flag if USER_INPUT is ambiguous about which collection should be used.

Do not create false alternatives that change the requested observable:

- Delta phi with MET uses the MET azimuthal direction. Do not treat "MET
  direction versus MET magnitude" as an ambiguity.
- Transverse mass is a scalar observable computed from its named inputs. Do not
  treat it as "a reconstructed composite object versus a scalar quantity".
- "Delta R between two named jets" means the delta R of those two jets. Do not
  reinterpret it as the minimum delta R over all jet pairs.
- A helper with a broader or different definition does not make that different
  definition a valid interpretation of USER_INPUT.
